- The AML rules, and their alerts ([#73](https://github.com/mdastpak/Crypto-Exchange/issues/73)). See [docs/aml.md](docs/aml.md).
  - `config/aml_rules.yaml`, built into the release, is checked key by key and synced at startup into `aml_rules`, with a version for each change kept in `aml_rule_history`; a rule that left the file is retired. A file that lists the country of the deployment as high-risk keeps the server from starting, and Iran is no longer in its lists. The file has a strict format now: `applies_to` names the operations, amounts are in USDT, and each type takes only its own settings. The rule that needed a machine-learning model is removed.
  - Each withdrawal is evaluated at both steps, in its transaction: velocity, structuring, high-risk and cross-border countries, unusual withdrawals, and dormant accounts, with amounts valued at the price engine's price; without a live price, a rule holds the withdrawal for review. A rule holds it (`review_reason: aml`), refuses it (`403 withdrawal_refused`, at step one before any code is sent), or refuses it and freezes the account, with the reason `aml_review`. While the rules cannot be evaluated, withdrawals answer `503 withdrawals_unavailable`.
  - A batch at `engine.evaluation_interval_seconds` raises the alerts of the velocity and structuring patterns that no single operation completed, once per window, and escalates the alerts left open for `engine.alert_escalation_hours`. The alerts that hold, refuse, or freeze, and the escalations, are emailed to the compliance team with `engine.notifications.email`.
  - `GET /admin/aml/rules`, `GET /admin/aml/alerts`, `GET /admin/aml/alerts/{id}`, and `POST /admin/aml/alerts/{id}/transition`: an alert is investigated, escalated, and resolved or dismissed with a resolution, audited as `aml.alert_status`.
  - Migration 50: the rules, their history, the operations evaluated, and the alerts are sealed from their first row; `withdrawals.review_reason` accepts `aml`; `integrity_findings` accepts the four tables.
  - The sanctions, mixer, darknet, and PEP rules are synced but not evaluated until a screening provider is configured, and the server names each at startup. Deposits and trades call the same evaluation once their paths wire it.
